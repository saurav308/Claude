## 0. The video in one breath

"Hydra ya farana?" is a brand question wearing a machine's clothes, and the video says so in the first minute: both are pick-and-carry cranes, two tiers of one class, named after two brands. Then it replaces the question with a process: list what you lift every day and at what reach; look at the ground it will stand on; read the load chart at your own radius to get the tonnage and the boom; buy it like the wheeled, registered machine it is, with a licensed operator and a written landed quote; and count the business by billed days, never by the day rate someone quoted. The rule: *ton se pehle din gino; load aur reach se ton; zameen se hydra ya farana.* Close on *Suno Sabki, Chuno Apni* and the brand principle.

## 1. Numbers policy for this video

- **Off-screen:** every price, EMI, day rate, the farana premium in rupees.
- **On-screen, allowed:** tonnage classes (12, 14, 15, 17, 20, 23, 25 tonnes as the market's ladder), boom reach in metres as a class fact, drive type, the shape of a load chart (capacity falls as radius grows), counts of billed days, the break-even-days *formula* without amounts.
- Source basis for the audit desk (site guide and model pages): hydra and farana are both pick-and-carry cranes; the hydra name comes from the Escorts Hydra range, farana from Franna (Terex); the hydra tier is two-wheel drive and lighter, the farana tier articulated with independent steering, mostly four-wheel drive, longer boom, heavier; the farana tier costs materially more for the same tonnage (stated relatively); a wheeled crane carries RTO registration, road tax, transport, insurance and TCS on top of the sticker; the rate card bills cranes by day or shift; waiting time is the crane business's silent loss.

**EN sub:** Step one: the lift list. One line for every daily lift: what, a slab, a pipe, a steel bundle, a girder. How heavy, roughly in tonnes. How many times a day. Circle the heaviest lift with its radius. That one decides the machine.

### S08 · 2:12–2:48 · Step 3: tonnage and boom from the load chart
**Shot:** a load chart on a cab plate (figures illegible, the curve clear); the host's finger slides from the minimum radius outward as the capacity bars shrink; the heaviest-lift line from S05 placed on the chart; the boom extended to the measured height.
**VO:** तीसरा step — ton और boom। crane का "चौदह ton" सबसे छोटे radius पर होता है — boom सीधा ऊपर, load बिल्कुल पास। radius बढ़ता है, capacity गिरती है — हर crane का chart यही दिखाता है। तो अपना सबसे भारी lift उसके radius पर chart में देखो — वहाँ जो capacity बची, वो आपके load से ऊपर होनी चाहिए। बारह, चौदह, पंद्रह, सत्रह, बीस, तेईस, पच्चीस — बाज़ार की ladder यही है; chart बताएगा आप कहाँ हो। और boom — आपकी नापी ऊँचाई तक पहुँचे, एक मंज़िल की गुंजाइश के साथ। ton और boom chart से निकलते हैं — दोस्त की सलाह से नहीं।
**EN sub:** Step three: tonnage and boom. A crane's "fourteen tonnes" is at its smallest radius, boom straight up, load right beside it. As the radius grows, capacity falls; every crane's chart shows this. So find your heaviest lift at its radius on the chart; the capacity left there must be above your load. Twelve, fourteen, fifteen, seventeen, twenty, twenty-three, twenty-five: that's the market's ladder; the chart tells you where you are. And the boom must reach your measured height, with one floor to spare. Tonnage and boom come from the chart, not from a friend's advice.
**Chips:** `RATED TONNAGE = AT MINIMUM RADIUS` · `CAPACITY FALLS AS RADIUS GROWS` · `READ THE CHART AT YOUR RADIUS` · `LADDER: 12 · 14 · 15 · 17 · 20 · 23 · 25 T` · `BOOM = YOUR HEIGHT + ONE FLOOR`

**VO:** चौथा step — ख़रीदना। crane पहियों पर है, सड़क पर चलती है — इसलिए RTO registration और road tax; ऊपर transport, insurance, TCS। dealer से landed quote लिखकर — हर line। operator: crane के लिए licence वाला operator चाहिए — पहले से तय करो कौन चलाएगा, training कहाँ से। service और parts — आपके ज़िले से कितनी दूर। और tier का सच: farana उसी ton पर काफ़ी महँगी है — वो four-wheel drive, मुड़ने वाली body और लंबा boom ख़रीद रहे हो। ज़मीन और reach माँगे, तभी।
**EN sub:** Step four: buying. A crane is on wheels and uses the road, so RTO registration and road tax; on top, transport, insurance, TCS. A landed quote from the dealer in writing, every line. The operator: a crane needs a licensed operator; decide beforehand who will drive it and where he'll train. Service and parts: how far from your district. And the truth about the tier: a farana is considerably dearer at the same tonnage; you're buying four-wheel drive, the bending body and the longer boom. Only when the ground and the reach demand it.
**Chips:** `ON-ROAD: + TRANSPORT + RTO + ROAD TAX + INSURANCE + TCS` · `LANDED QUOTE IN WRITING` · `LICENSED OPERATOR · TRAINING` · `SERVICE + PARTS DISTANCE` · `FARANA PREMIUM: ONLY IF GROUND OR REACH DEMANDS`

### S10 · 3:20–3:52 · Step 5: earning by billed days (method)
**Shot:** a calendar with billed days ticked and waiting days crossed; a crane idling beside an empty truck bay; a contract page with a "waiting hours" clause highlighted; the break-even formula card without amounts.
**VO:** पाँचवाँ step — कमाई। crane दिन या shift से bill होती है। और सबसे ज़रूरी number दिन का rate नहीं — महीने में कितने दिन bill हुए। crane का आधा दिन इंतज़ार में जाता है — truck नहीं आया, slab तैयार नहीं। इंतज़ार का पैसा कौन देगा, contract में लिखो। और break-even का तरीका: महीने की EMI plus fixed ख़र्च — operator, insurance, maintenance का provision — भाग अपने ज़िले का दिन का rate — उतने दिन चाहिए हर महीने। वो number अपने असली billed दिनों से मिलाओ। rate पड़ोसी से मत लो — अपने ज़िले में तीन लोगों से पूछो।
**EN sub:** Step five: earning. A crane is billed by the day or the shift. And the most important number isn't the day rate; it's how many days a month got billed. Half a crane's day goes in waiting: the truck didn't come, the slab wasn't ready. Write who pays for waiting into the contract. And the break-even method: the month's EMI plus fixed costs, operator, insurance, maintenance provision, divided by your district's day rate: that's the days you need every month. Set that against your real billed days. Don't take the rate from a neighbour; ask three people in your own district.
**Chips:** `BILLED BY DAY / SHIFT` · `BILLED DAYS > RATE` · `WAITING: WHO PAYS? IN THE CONTRACT` · `BREAK-EVEN DAYS = (EMI + FIXED COSTS) ÷ DAY RATE`
