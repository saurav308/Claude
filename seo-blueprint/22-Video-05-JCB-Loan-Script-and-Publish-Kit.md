## 0. The video in one breath

A machine loan is the one part of buying a machine where the buyer does the least preparation and pays for it the longest. The video turns it into six steps the buyer controls: build the file before you walk in; choose the kind of lender by what you need, speed or cost; read the offer by its four questions; size the EMI from your worst month, not your best; check five things before you sign; and run the loan after, with a buffer and a protocol for a bad month. No rate is quoted, because rates change every quarter; the questions do not. The rule: *EMI utni, jo barsaat ke mahine mein bhi nikal jaaye, aur sign se pehle Key Fact Statement.* Close on the brand principle: *machine ke saath emotions hone chahiye, machine ko leke emotional nahi.*

---

## 1. Numbers policy for this video

- **Off-screen:** interest rates, EMI amounts, funding percentages, fee percentages, CIBIL cut-off numbers, scheme terms with dates. The description links to the finance desk and the guides where current bands live.
- **On-screen, allowed:** the coverage *ratio* lenders test (about one and a quarter times, a durable benchmark), the count of months of buffer (two), the shape of a reducing balance vs a flat charge (a mechanism, not a rate), the list of documents, the rules on bounce charges (regulatory principles, not amounts).
- Source basis for the audit desk (from the site's finance guides): lenders fund a share of the machine, not the bill; OEM schemes fund more of the machine cost and leave extras in cash; a flat rate charges on the full principal for the whole tenure while a reducing rate charges on the outstanding balance; longer tenure lowers the instalment and raises the total; CIBIL has bands with a comfortable zone and a hard zone; lenders look for a coverage near 1.25×; penal charges on a bounce cannot be penal interest, cannot carry interest, cannot stack, and apply only to the amount in default; a loan against one machine to pay another's EMI is a delay, not a fix.

### S06 · 1:40–2:16 · Step 3: read the offer, four questions (cutaway)
**Shot:** the offer sheet; four lines highlighted as asked. X-ray ≤8 s: a reducing balance stepping down month by month with the interest slice shrinking, beside a flat block where the slice never shrinks. A calendar stretching for tenure.
**VO:** तीसरा step — offer पढ़ो, चार सवाल। पहला: कितना प्रतिशत — किस चीज़ का? machine की कीमत का या on-road का? lender machine का हिस्सा देता है — insurance, transport, registration, पहली EMI — आपकी जेब से। "zero down" भी ज़्यादातर यही होता है। दूसरा: rate reducing है या flat? flat पूरे loan पर पूरी tenure byaaj लेता है — जो चुका दिया उस पर भी। हमेशा reducing rate और total payable माँगो। तीसरा: tenure। लंबी tenure — छोटी EMI, बड़ा total। चौथा: fee — processing, foreclosure, और EMI छुट्टी के महीनों में byaaj चलता है या नहीं। "पहली दो EMI आधी" — तो बाक़ी आधी कहाँ गई? पूछो।
**EN sub:** Step three: read the offer, four questions. First: what percent, of what? Of the machine price or of on-road? The lender funds a share of the machine; insurance, transport, registration and the first EMI come from your pocket. "Zero down" is mostly this too. Second: is the rate reducing or flat? A flat rate charges interest on the whole loan for the whole tenure, even on what you've repaid. Always ask for the reducing rate and the total payable. Third: tenure. Longer tenure, smaller EMI, bigger total. Fourth: fees. Processing, foreclosure, and whether interest runs through EMI-holiday months. "First two EMIs at half": then where did the other half go? Ask.
**Chips:** `Q1 % OF WHAT: MACHINE OR ON-ROAD` · `Q2 REDUCING OR FLAT → ASK TOTAL PAYABLE` · `Q3 TENURE: SMALLER EMI, BIGGER TOTAL` · `Q4 FEES · HOLIDAY INTEREST · WHERE THE HALF WENT`

**VO:** चौथा step — EMI का size। dealer पूछेगा "कितनी EMI निकाल लोगे?" — और आप अपने अच्छे महीने से जवाब दोगे। ग़लत महीना। card पर लिखो: barsaat के महीने में machine कितने दिन चलेगी, उस महीने की कमाई; उसमें से maintenance, insurance, parking — बचा हुआ, वही आपकी EMI की सीमा। EMI उससे ऊपर जाए — तो down payment बढ़ाओ, EMI नहीं खींचो। और bank में दो महीने के fixed ख़र्च का buffer — पहले दिन से। loan वो नहीं जो अच्छे महीने में निकले। loan वो, जो बुरे महीने में भी निकल जाए।
**EN sub:** Step four: size the EMI. The dealer will ask "how much EMI can you manage?" and you'll answer from your good month. Wrong month. Write on the card: how many days the machine will work in the monsoon month, that month's income; take out maintenance, insurance, parking; what's left is your EMI ceiling. If the EMI goes above it, raise the down payment, don't stretch the EMI. And a buffer of two months' fixed costs in the bank, from day one. A loan isn't the one that clears in a good month. It's the one that clears in the bad month.

### S10 · 3:45–4:20 · Step 5: five checks before you sign
**Shot:** a Key Fact Statement page, the charge line highlighted; an insurance policy with the lender's name as assignee; an RC/hypothecation stamp; the host holds the pen back (#39).
**VO:** पाँचवाँ step — sign करने से पहले पाँच check। एक — Key Fact Statement माँगो: rate, fee, bounce charge, सब एक पन्ने पर; नहीं दिया, तो sign नहीं। दो — bounce का नियम समझो: EMI छूटे तो lender penal charge लगा सकता है — penal interest नहीं; उस charge पर byaaj नहीं, penalty पर penalty नहीं, और charge सिर्फ़ छूटी रकम पर। तीन — insurance: policy में lender का नाम होगा — premium कौन भरेगा, कब। चार — hypothecation और RC: machine के कागज़ पर lender का हक़ loan तक रहेगा — पूरा चुकने पर हटवाना आपका काम। पाँच — EMI की तारीख़: आपके bill आने की तारीख़ के बाद रखो, पहले नहीं।
**EN sub:** Step five: five checks before you sign. One: ask for the Key Fact Statement: rate, fees, bounce charge, all on one page; if they won't give it, don't sign. Two: understand the bounce rule: if an EMI is missed the lender may levy a penal charge, not penal interest; no interest on that charge, no penalty on a penalty, and only on the amount missed. Three: insurance: the lender's name will be on the policy; who pays the premium, and when. Four: hypothecation and the RC: the lender's claim stays on the machine's papers until the loan ends; removing it afterwards is your job. Five: the EMI date: set it after your billing date, not before.
**Chips:** `1 KEY FACT STATEMENT` · `2 BOUNCE = PENAL CHARGE, NOT INTEREST · ONLY ON THE MISSED AMOUNT` · `3 INSURANCE ASSIGNMENT` · `4 HYPOTHECATION · RC` · `5 EMI DATE AFTER BILLING DATE`

**Shot:** an auto-debit SMS; a two-month buffer envelope; a phone call to the lender before the due date; a greyed "loan on machine A to pay machine B" card crossed out.

**EN sub:** Step six: running the loan. Set up auto-debit, but keep the buffer in that account, two months' worth. If a bad month is coming, talk to the lender before the EMI bounces, not after; a month's grace can be had, a missed EMI costs more. And one mistake never: a loan on one machine to pay another machine's EMI. That's not a fix, it's a delay, and next time two machines' EMIs bounce.
**Chips:** `AUTO-DEBIT + BUFFER IN THE SAME ACCOUNT` · `BAD MONTH → CALL BEFORE THE DUE DATE` · `NEVER: LOAN ON MACHINE A TO PAY MACHINE B`
