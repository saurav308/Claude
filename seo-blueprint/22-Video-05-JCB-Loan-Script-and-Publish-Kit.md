## 0. The video in one breath

The finance desk slides one paper across the table: *100% finance*. The buyer hears "not a rupee from my pocket". The video does the hisaab on that one paper. Lenders fund a share of the *machine*, not of the bill: insurance, transport, registration and the first EMI are still cash. One JCB 3DX, one ₹24 lakh loan, three rates that are all being quoted in the market today, and the EMI moves from ₹49,000 to ₹57,000 a month, a gap of ₹4.7 lakh over five years, a mini excavator's down payment. Then the two traps (a "flat" rate and a seven-year tenure), what makes a file pass (CIBIL, papers, the one coverage number a bank actually tests), what the law allows when an EMI bounces, and the rule: *the EMI must clear in the monsoon month, not the best month.* Close on the brand principle: *machine ke saath emotions hone chahiye, machine ko leke emotional nahi.*

### S09 · 2:46–3:10 · Trap one: the flat rate (cutaway)
**Shot (#24):** ≤8 s animation: a reducing balance stepping down month by month with interest shrinking, beside a flat block where interest stays full-height; chips land.
**VO:** तीसरी बात — दो जाल। पहला: "flat rate"। कोई कहे — सात प्रतिशत flat — सुनने में bank से सस्ता। flat rate पूरे loan पर पूरे पाँच साल byaaj लगाता है — जो चुका दिया उस पर भी। reducing rate सिर्फ़ बचे हुए पर। पाँच साल के loan में flat, reducing का लगभग पौने दो गुना होता है। सात flat — मतलब क़रीब तेरह reducing। हमेशा पूछो: reducing rate क्या है, और total कितना चुकाना है।
**EN sub:** Third: two traps. The first: the "flat rate". Someone says seven percent flat, and it sounds cheaper than the bank. A flat rate charges interest on the whole loan for all five years, even on what you've already repaid. A reducing rate only on what's left. Over five years a flat rate is roughly 1.8 times the reducing one. Seven flat means about thirteen reducing. Always ask: what is the reducing rate, and what is the total I repay.
**Chips:** `FLAT ≈ 1.8 × REDUCING` · `7% FLAT ≈ 13% REDUCING` · `ASK: REDUCING RATE + TOTAL PAYABLE`

### S12 · 4:25–5:00 · How a file passes
**Shot:** a CIBIL score dial; a neat eight-page file on a desk; the one-number card building: income → cash → EMI → ratio.
**VO:** चौथी बात — file। पहला दरवाज़ा CIBIL: सात सौ पचास से ऊपर — आसान, best rate। सात सौ से — आम तौर पर हाँ। छह सौ पचास से सात सौ — co-applicant या ज़्यादा down payment। उससे नीचे — bank मुश्किल, NBFC महँगा। कागज़: Aadhaar-PAN, पता, GST या business proof, छह से बारह महीने का bank statement, ITR, और उसी model की dealer quotation। और एक number जो bank असल में देखता है: machine की साल की कमाई, EMI से सवा गुना होनी चाहिए। ग्यारह महीने नब्बे हज़ार का dry hire — नौ लाख नब्बे हज़ार। maintenance, insurance, admin घटाओ — सात लाख नब्बे हज़ार बचे। साल की EMI छह लाख चौरानवे हज़ार। ratio — एक दशमलव एक चार। bank को सवा चाहिए। एक महीना और bill करो — एक दशमलव दो सात। पूरा business इस एक line में है।
**EN sub:** Fourth: the file. The first door is CIBIL: above 750, easy and the best rate. From 700, usually a yes. 650 to 700, a co-applicant or a bigger down payment. Below that, hard with a bank, expensive with an NBFC. Papers: Aadhaar and PAN, address, GST or business proof, six to twelve months of bank statements, ITRs, and the dealer quotation for that exact model. And the one number the bank really tests: the machine's yearly earnings should be one and a quarter times the EMI. Eleven months of dry hire at ninety thousand: 9.9 lakh. Take off maintenance, insurance, admin: 7.9 lakh left. The year's EMI: 6.94 lakh. Ratio 1.14. The bank wants 1.25. Bill one more month: 1.27. The whole business is in that one line.
**Chips:** `CIBIL 750+ · 700+ · 650–699 · <650` · `PAPERS: ID · ADDRESS · GST · 6–12 MO STATEMENTS · ITR · QUOTATION` · `COVERAGE: ₹7.9 L ÷ ₹6.94 L = 1.14 · BANK WANTS 1.25`
