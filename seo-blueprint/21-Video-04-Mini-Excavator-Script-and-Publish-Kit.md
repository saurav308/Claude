## 0. The video in one breath

A first-time buyer chooses a machine the way he chooses a phone: by what everyone else has and what it costs today. Both change. What does not change is the work he will do for the next twelve months, the width of the gate it must pass through, and the ground it will stand on. The video walks the five-step process that turns those facts into a machine: write the work, measure the gate, match the size, count the running costs with your own numbers, then test it on your own site and ask the dealer three questions. Along the way it kills the one assumption that sends buyers wrong: a mini is not the *cheap* machine, it is the *small* machine, and a small machine is bought for the work, never for the price. The rule: *gate se naapo, kaam se chuno.* Close on the brand principle: *hum bechte nahi, aapko kharidne mein help karte hain.*

## 1. Numbers policy for this video (new, applies to the whole batch)

- **Off-screen:** rupee amounts, EMIs, interest and tax rates, hire rates, dated offers, "price in 2026". All of it lives on the paired page and in one description line: *aaj ke price bands page par.*
- **On-screen, allowed:** physical facts that define the machine class (weight class in tonnes, "digs about three metres", track width "under a metre" vs "over seven feet" for a backhoe), counts (days, hours, batches), ratios and methods (litres per hour measured by the top-up method, "machine width plus one foot").
- **Relative statements replace prices:** "a three-ton mini and a two-wheel-drive backhoe cost about the same" is durable; the numbers behind it are not.
- Physical facts used (from the site's mini guide and model pages, for the audit desk's check): minis sold in India run roughly 1.8 to 5.8 tonnes, dig about 2.3 to 3.8 metres, carry 13 to 49 HP; a 3-ton mini is about five feet wide and some retract their tracks to under a metre; a backhoe is over seven and a half feet wide and digs around four and a half metres with a bucket roughly three times a 3-ton mini's; tracked machines are not RTO-registered (**confirm with ops**); a new operator settles into a mini in two or three days.
